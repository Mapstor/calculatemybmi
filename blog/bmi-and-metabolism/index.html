BMI and Metabolism: The Complete Guide to Metabolic Health

Written by Marko Visic, MPharm · Not medical advice

Metabolism turns food into energy, and the energy your body uses at rest is the basal metabolic rate (ATA). Bigger bodies burn more, not less: total daily energy expenditure rises with fat-free mass (Pontzer et al., 2021). Below: the BMR equations, how BMI relates to metabolic rate and metabolic health, muscle, age, the thyroid, and what weight loss does to the energy you burn.

What Is Metabolism?

Metabolism refers to all the chemical processes that occur within your body to maintain life. These processes convert the food you eat into energy that powers everything from breathing and circulation to cell repair and physical movement.

TEF

Thermic Effect of Food

Energy expended digesting, absorbing, and processing the nutrients in your food.

PA

Physical Activity

Energy used during exercise and non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT) like walking, fidgeting, and daily tasks.

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) Explained

Basal Metabolic Rate is the minimum number of calories your body needs to perform essential functions while completely at rest. This includes maintaining body temperature, powering brain function, supporting organ function, and enabling cell production and repair.

BMR Calculation Formulas

Published equations estimate resting energy expenditure from weight, height, age and sex. Mifflin and colleagues measured it in 498 adults and found that the original Harris-Benedict equations of 1919 overestimated it by 5% (Mifflin et al., 1990). Two equations in use:

Both equations use total weight, so neither can tell muscle from fat. In Mifflin’s data, fat-free mass was the best single predictor of resting energy expenditure (Mifflin et al., 1990); the lean body mass calculator estimates it.

How BMI Relates to Metabolic Rate

People with a higher BMI typically burn more energy in absolute terms, because total expenditure rises with fat-free mass (Pontzer et al., 2021).

BMI vs. Metabolic Health Indicators

Burning more does not mean healthier markers. Overweight and obesity raise the risk of high blood pressure, high blood cholesterol and high blood glucose (NIDDK), and people with obesity often have higher blood levels of insulin and chronic inflammation (NCI). The risks that go with each BMI range are in BMI and health risks; what BMI misses is in BMI limitations.

Prevalence: 34.7% of US adults, NHANES 2011–2016 (Hirode & Wong, JAMA 2020). Risk: metabolic syndrome confers about a 5-fold increase in type 2 diabetes risk and twice the risk of cardiovascular disease over 5–10 years (Alberti et al., Circulation 2009).

A diagnosis needs at least three of five conditions: a large waist, high triglycerides, high blood pressure, high fasting blood glucose and low HDL cholesterol. NIDDK says metabolic syndrome is closely linked to overweight and obesity and to a lack of physical activity (NIDDK). Our how to lower BMI guide has CDC’s steps.

Metabolically Healthy Obesity: Can You Be Fit But Fat?

Definition
Metabolically healthy obesity (MHO): obesity without metabolic disease. Definitions vary; a study of 3.5 million UK adults counted a BMI of 30 or more with none of three abnormalities: diabetes, hypertension and hyperlipidaemia (Caleyachetty et al., 2017).

The MHO Controversy

Over a mean 5.4 years, people in that study with obesity and none of the three had a higher risk of coronary heart disease (hazard ratio 1.49), cerebrovascular disease (1.07) and heart failure (1.96) than people of healthy weight with none (Caleyachetty et al., 2017). So “metabolically healthy” did not mean free of heart risk. The healthy BMI range guide explains the categories.

Normal Weight Metabolically Unhealthy: The "Skinny Fat" Phenomenon

The reverse group exists too. “Normal weight metabolically unhealthy” (NWMU), sometimes called “skinny fat”, means a BMI in the healthy range with metabolic abnormalities.

In the same 3.5 million adults, the risk of coronary heart disease, cerebrovascular disease and heart failure rose with the number of metabolic abnormalities in people of healthy weight as well as in those with overweight or obesity (Caleyachetty et al., 2017).

CDC says that for individuals, BMI should be considered with other factors, such as blood pressure and cholesterol levels (CDC). Our guide on body fat vs BMI goes further.

How Muscle Mass Affects Metabolism

At rest, a kilogram of skeletal muscle uses about 13 kcal a day and a kilogram of fat about 4.5, in the organ-by-organ rates Wang and colleagues validated across adulthood (Wang et al., 2010).

Building and Keeping Muscle

Muscle-Strengthening Activity

The Physical Activity Guidelines ask adults for muscle-strengthening activity, like lifting weights or doing push-ups, at least 2 days each week (Physical Activity Guidelines), and CDC counts stronger bones and muscles among the benefits of regular activity (CDC).

Protein Foods

CDC’s healthy eating plan, from the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, includes protein foods such as seafood, meats, poultry, eggs, beans and peas, soy products, nuts and seeds (CDC).

Age-Related Metabolic Changes and BMI

Pontzer and colleagues measured daily energy expenditure from 8 days to 95 years of age in 29 countries. Adjusted for fat-free mass, it climbed to about 50% above adult values around age 1, fell slowly to adult levels by about 20, held steady from 20 to 60, even in pregnancy, and declined after that (Pontzer et al., 2021).

Why Energy Use Falls With Age

Muscle mass falls by an estimated 3–8% per decade after age 30 (Volpi et al., 2004), and total daily expenditure rises and falls with fat-free mass (Pontzer et al., 2021). Our BMI by age guide explains how BMI is read at different ages.

Thyroid Function and BMI

The thyroid, a butterfly-shaped gland in the lower front of the neck, makes hormones the body relies on to use energy and keep warm. In thyroid disorders, high hormone levels raise the basal metabolic rate and low levels lower it, though BMR is only part of the story for weight (American Thyroid Association).

Important Note
For most patients, about 5 to 10 pounds of weight gain may be related to an underactive thyroid, depending on how severe it is, and most of that gain is salt and water (American Thyroid Association).

How Losing Weight Affects Metabolism

As weight falls, so does the energy the body burns, so the same intake brings less loss over time; in Hall and colleagues’ model, half of the eventual change from a new intake comes in about a year (Hall 2011). Our how to lower BMI guide covers the arithmetic.

Metabolic Adaptation

The drop can be larger than the lost weight explains. In 14 contestants from “The Biggest Loser”, who lost 58.3 kg on average in a 30-week competition, resting metabolic rate six years later was 704 kcal a day below where it started, and 499 kcal a day below what their body composition and age predicted, although they had regained 41.0 kg on average. The authors call the adaptation persistent and likely a proportional but incomplete response to efforts to keep weight down (Fothergill et al., 2016).

Keeping Weight Off

  • Gradual loss: people who lose about 1 to 2 pounds a week are more likely to keep the weight off than people who lose weight quicker (CDC)
  • Regular activity: most weight loss comes from eating fewer calories, but regular physical activity is the only way to maintain it (CDC)
  • A plan from your current weight: NIDDK’s Body Weight Planner works out calories and activity to reach a goal weight and keep it

Raising the Energy You Burn

More physical activity raises the number of calories your body uses (CDC); CDC’s calories table, in our how to lower BMI guide, shows how much common activities use. At rest, muscle uses about 13 kcal per kg a day against 4.5 for fat (Wang et al., 2010), and the Physical Activity Guidelines ask for muscle-strengthening activity at least 2 days each week (Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans).
