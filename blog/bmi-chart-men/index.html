BMI Chart for Men: Healthy Ranges, Weight Tables, and Male-Specific Guidance

Reference chart · Not medical advice

This page is a reference chart, not a calculator. Find the row for your height and read across to see which weight ranges land in each BMI category. BMI cut-offs are the same for adult men as for adult women — the chart on this page is presented in the height range most common for men, from 5'2" to 6'6". For an instant calculation, use the BMI calculator for men.

A healthy BMI for adult men is 18.5 to 24.9, matching the category range published by the CDC adult BMI categories. That band is highlighted green for every height row on the chart. A BMI below 18.5 is classified as underweight and a BMI of 25 or higher is classified as overweight or obese.

BMI chart for men by height and weight

Each row is a specific height; the coloured cells show the weight range that produces a BMI in each CDC category at that height. Weights are in pounds (with kilograms in parentheses). Every listed weight is guaranteed to be inside its category once BMI is rounded to 0.1 half-up.

How to read this chart

  1. Find your height row on the left. Each row has a stable anchor (for example #h-5-9) so you can link directly to a height.
  2. Read across to see the weight range for each BMI category at that height.
  3. Match your weight to the coloured band it falls into. Bands are inclusive of both endpoints in pounds.
  4. If you want the exact BMI number rather than a category, use the BMI calculator for men.

Does the healthy BMI range change with age?

CDC and WHO adult BMI cut-offs do not shift with age — 18.5 to 24.9 remains the healthy range across the adult lifespan. Observational research (Winter et al., Am J Clin Nutr, 2014) reports a mortality plateau at somewhat higher BMI values (roughly 23 to 33) in adults aged 65 and older, but that is an observed pattern rather than a recommendation. See the BMI by age guide for the full discussion.

Methodology

  • Formula: BMI = weight in pounds × 703 ÷ height in inches squared. The metric form is weight in kilograms ÷ height in metres squared.
  • Rounding rule: BMI is rounded to one decimal place, half-up. Every weight is categorised by its rounded BMI, and the band shown is the lowest to the highest whole pound that produces a BMI in that category at the given height.
  • Category cut-offs: underweight below 18.5; healthy weight 18.5 to less than 25; overweight 25 to less than 30; obesity 30 or greater; class 1 30 to less than 35; class 2 35 to less than 40; class 3 (severe) 40 or greater — per the CDC adult BMI categories page.
  • "Calculated": every cell in the chart is calculated from the formula and rounding rule above — no numbers on this page are hand-authored.

Frequently asked questions

A healthy BMI for an adult man is 18.5 to 24.9. The cut-offs are the same for men and women.

The chart cut-offs remain 18.5 to 24.9. BMI does not distinguish muscle from fat, so highly muscular men may land in the overweight band despite low body fat. If that describes you, see the BMI for athletes and body fat vs BMI guides.
