Findings, all settled before packaging:
- **FAIL, the ratio was rounded before the ADA test (fixed).** At 5′4″ a 31.7-inch waist (ratio 0.4953) printed 0.50 and got "counts as obesity with a waist at least half the height, and yours is"; the same at 162 cm with 80.5 cm. Every waist from 0.495 × height up to half the height was affected (1,320 ADA sentences and 236 Asian-background lines in the grid), against the page's own height table and statistics, which use the unrounded ratio. The check now rounds the waist to 0.1 in or 0.1 cm, works the ratio out from that printed waist and cuts it to two decimals (`Math.floor(r*100+1e-9)/100`): 0.4953 shows as 0.49, so 0.50 always means at least half the height, and NICE's classes and the ADA test read the same number. The worked example shows 0.51 (33 ÷ 64 = 0.516).
- **FAIL, the imperial waist check contradicted itself (fixed).** At 5′11″ with 34.8 in (88.4 cm) and BMI 25.0 one box said the waist was "not over 35 in (88 cm)" and then "88 cm (about 35 in) or more, and yours is". The US-guidelines sentence now names only the unit it compared ("over 35 in", or "over 88 cm" in metric; the method says the guidelines give both lines); the ADA sentences quote the waist in cm against 88 cm ("and yours, at 88.4 cm, is"); "about 35 in" is gone and 88 cm is given as 34.6 in everywhere else.
- **FAIL, one table cell (fixed).** 5′5″ half height read 82.5 cm: 65 × 2.54 ÷ 2 = 82.55, which float formatting printed down. Every cm figure the generator prints for heights and half heights is now worked out in exact decimals and rounded half up (`dcm()`), so 82.6, as in the FAQ. Chat found the same slip in the calculator's waist conversion (`toFixed`: 32.5 in printed as 82.5 cm, 210 grid cases); it now uses the page's half-up rounding.
