## Independent fact-check (separate agent, on the draft)
Recomputed from the raw NHANES files with its own code: about 130 survey figures (the category shares, CIs and sample sizes for all adults, men and women; the sex differences; BMI under 20; height coverage; the 2026 ADA comparison with its 5,875 adults, 54 left out, 30.7-point gap and 27.9 / 2.8 split; the 94.5% waist share; the age shares; the DXA medians), 1,247 formula values (all chart cells in lb and kg, the heights-for-each-weight tables, the 483 printable-table values, 161 of them read off the image) and 59 row labels, and matched all 600 plotted dots to real NHANES adults. Nothing differed. Its sandbox could not open the web, so chat checked the outside sources: the ADA press release (June 29, 2026) and ADA Meeting News (the screening-and-diagnosis chapter is new in June 2026; the first chapters came out in 2025), CDC's About BMI page (the muscle sentence, verbatim), and the registry rows verified 2026-10-14 to 2026-10-16 for the rest.
Its findings, all settled before packaging:
- FAIL: the 62 kg answer left out that 143 cm or less is obesity. Added.
- 6 warnings: "added the waist in June 2026" now reads "published a new chapter of its obesity standards that adds the waist" (a 2025 edition existed; the chapter is new); a note explains why BMI alone gives 40.6% in the ADA comparison and 40.3% across all adults (the 54 adults left out all have a BMI under 30); "matching NCHS's published crude rates"; the Asian thresholds spelled out in the FAQ; the figures draw the category edges at the rounding lines, so the dot counts in the description match the picture; the CDC muscle quote checked verbatim (no change).
- 8 notes: the all-adult median removed (NCHS publishes medians by sex only, and the weighted median sits on a rounding edge, 28.2 or 28.3); "about 204 lb"; "±4.7 points"; 203 lb marked as BMI 30.0 rounded to one decimal; the printable table now says your BMI is the column's number "or a little more" (page and image); the Article author and publisher are named on the page (also on the healthy-range guide and the women's chart). No change needed for CDC's "18.5 to less than 25" (the same range at one decimal) or for the 54 adults left out (disclosed in the method).
