BMI Chart for Adults: Height and Weight Chart

Written by Marko Visic, MPharm · Not medical advice

What should you weigh for your height? Which BMI does your weight give, without a calculator? And is there a new chart for 2026?

Only 26.0% of US adults have a BMI in CDC’s healthy range of 18.5 to 24.9 (our calculation from CDC measurements, 2021–2023). Since June 2026, the American Diabetes Association also counts the waist, and 71.4% of US adults meet its obesity thresholds, against 40.6% of the same adults by BMI alone (our calculation).

Below: the chart for every height from 4′10″ to 6′6″ in pounds and kilograms, a printable BMI table, the heights at which a weight is healthy, and the 2026 waist rule.

Key takeaways

A BMI chart gives the weight range for each BMI category at your height. CDC’s healthy range is a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9 for every adult, women and men, at any age. At 5′9″ that is 125 to 168 lb (57 to 76 kg); 169 to 202 lb is overweight and 203 lb or more is obesity.

  • About 1 in 4 US adults has a healthy BMI (26.0%); 32.1% are overweight and 40.3% have obesity (our calculation from CDC measurements, 2021–2023, matching NCHS’s published crude rates).
  • The 2026 waist rule: at a BMI of 25 to 29.9, the American Diabetes Association counts a waist of at least half your height toward obesity, and 94.5% of US adults in that range have one (our calculation).
  • Under 20, BMI is read as a percentile for age and sex, not on this chart.

Medical disclaimer: This page is for information only. BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis. Ask a healthcare professional for medical advice.

How to read a BMI chart

  1. Find your height in the left column. Centimeters are under each height.
  2. Read across to the cell that holds your weight. Its column is your BMI category.
  3. For the BMI number, use the printable BMI table or the BMI calculator.

Example. Say you’re 5′9″ and weigh 185 lb (83.9 kg). The overweight cell in that row runs from 169 to 202 lb, so 185 lb is overweight: a BMI of 27.3 (185 × 703 ÷ 69², rounded to one decimal; CDC formula). The healthy range at 5′9″ is 125 to 168 lb, so 185 lb is 17 lb above it.

Why other charts give different numbers

NIH’s printable BMI table has one column for each whole BMI from 19 to 54. At 5′9″ its BMI 19 column reads 128 lb and its BMI 24 column 162 lb, so a chart read from those two columns gives 128 to 162 lb. CDC’s healthy range runs from 18.5 to 24.9, and at 5′9″ that is 125 to 168 lb. Where NIH’s columns start a category, at BMI 25 and 30, its weights match this chart: 169 and 203 lb at 5′9″.

“Ideal weight” charts differ for another reason: they give one weight per height from a formula, not a range. The ideal weight calculator shows four of them.

Printable BMI table

The table gives the BMI for every height from 4′10″ to 6′6″. Find your height and move across to the heaviest weight that is not above yours: your BMI is the number at the top of that column or a little more. A weight below the 18.5 column is underweight.

Each cell is the lightest whole pound whose BMI, rounded to one decimal, reaches the number at the top of the column, so the 18.5, 25, 30, 35 and 40 columns match the category limits in the chart above. NIH’s table lists one weight for each whole BMI and can differ by a pound: at 5′9″ and BMI 19, NIH gives 128 lb and this table 129 lb, because 128 lb is a BMI of 18.9.

The 2026 waist rule

A BMI chart can’t show where fat sits. In June 2026 the American Diabetes Association published a new chapter of its obesity standards that adds the waist to the diagnosis of obesity in adults who aren’t pregnant (ADA). CDC’s categories haven’t changed.

  • BMI 30 or more: obesity, as on the chart.
  • BMI 25 to 29.9: obesity when the waist is at least half the height, or 88 cm (about 35 in) or more in women and 102 cm (about 40 in) or more in men; otherwise overweight.
  • Adults of Asian background: obesity from a BMI of 27.5, or from 23 with a waist at least half the height, or 80 cm (31.5 in) or more in women and 90 cm (35.5 in) or more in men (ADA, 2026).

Either waist test is enough, so the lower line decides. For most adults that is half the height: 30.0 in (76.2 cm) at 5′0″, 34.5 in (87.6 cm) at 5′9″ and 36.0 in (91.4 cm) at 6′0″. For women 5′10″ and taller it is 88 cm. For adults of Asian background it is 80 cm for women 5′3″ and taller, and 90 cm for men 5′11″ and taller.

The 54 adults left out of this comparison, whose class depends on a waist that wasn’t measured, all have a BMI under 30. That is why BMI alone gives 40.6% here and 40.3% across all 5,929 adults.

Most of the gap is adults with a BMI of 25 to 29.9: 94.5% of them have a waist at least half their height. Non-Asian adults in that range account for 27.9 percentage points of the difference, and adults of Asian background with a BMI of 23 to 29.9 for 2.8. By sex: 73.4% of men against 39.5%, and 69.4% of women against 41.8% (the BMI chart for women has the detail).

In January 2025 a Lancet commission, endorsed by 76 organizations, also proposed confirming excess body fat with a waist measure or a direct body-fat measurement in addition to BMI (Rubino et al., 2025). The waist-to-height ratio guide shows how to measure your waist.

Age, sex and children

One chart for every adult. CDC sets adult BMI categories “regardless of age, sex, or race” (CDC), so the chart is the same for women and men at 20 or at 80. The body behind the number differs:

  • Sex. At a healthy BMI, the median US woman aged 20 to 59 had 33.4% body fat and the median man 22.1% (our analysis of CDC body scans, 2011–2018). The BMI chart for women and the BMI chart for men cover each sex’s usual heights.
  • Muscle. “BMI does not distinguish between fat, muscle, and bone mass” (CDC), so a very muscular adult can read as overweight.
  • Age. “Loss of muscle mass in older adults reduces the accuracy of BMI, which may lead to underdiagnosis of obesity” (ADA, 2026). BMI by age covers older adults.

Children and teens. Under 20, BMI is read as a percentile for age and sex, not on this chart (CDC); the BMI calculator for children and teens works it out for ages 2 to 19.

Pregnancy. During pregnancy, CDC’s advice on weight gain depends on BMI before pregnancy; the BMI chart for women has the table.

Sources and method

Written by Marko Visic, MPharm. “Our calculation” means we computed the figure from CDC’s public survey files with the method below.

  • CDC. Adult BMI Categories: the adult cut-offs and the “regardless of age, sex, or race” rule (page last reviewed March 2024).
  • CDC. About Body Mass Index (BMI): what BMI does and doesn’t measure.
  • CDC. Child and Teen BMI Categories: percentiles for ages 2 to 19.
  • CDC. Calculating BMI: the formulas in US units and metric.
  • CDC. Weight Gain During Pregnancy: recommended gain by BMI before pregnancy (Institute of Medicine).
  • NIH (NHLBI). Body Mass Index Table (NIH Publication No. 22-HL-8190, September 2022): one column for each whole BMI from 19 to 54.
  • CDC NHANES August 2021–August 2023 demographics and body measures files (DEMO_L, BMX_L): our calculation of the share of adults aged 20 and over in each BMI category (5,929 adults), of the 2026 ADA thresholds (5,875 adults), of BMI under 20 and of heights from 4′10″ to 6′6″, weighted with the exam weights, pregnant women excluded; the 600-adult figure is a sample drawn from the same files. The same files give the average weight at each height on average weight by height.
  • Emmerich SD, et al. NCHS Data Brief No. 508 (Sep 2024): obesity and severe obesity in US adults by sex and age, August 2021–August 2023.
  • Fryar CD, Afful J, Saif NT. NCHS Health E-Stat 111 (Feb 2026): overweight and obesity in US adults, August 2021–August 2023.
  • American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Obesity. Screening, Diagnosis, Evaluation, and Staging of Obesity in Adults: Standards of Care in Overweight and Obesity—2026: obesity thresholds with waist size (recommendations 3.3a and 3.3b); ADA press release, June 2026.
  • Rubino F, et al. Definition and diagnostic criteria of clinical obesity (Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology, 2025): the Lancet Commission on Clinical Obesity.
  • CDC NHANES 2011–2018 DXA body scans: our analysis of body fat by BMI band, women and men aged 20 to 59 (body fat percentage chart).

Method. Charts: the lightest and heaviest whole pounds (or kilograms) whose BMI, rounded to one decimal, falls in each CDC category at each height, each unit worked out on its own; BMI = lb × 703 ÷ in², or kg ÷ m². Printable table: the lightest whole pound whose rounded BMI reaches each column. Heights for each weight: every whole inch from 4′10″ to 6′6″ and every whole centimeter from 145 to 200 cm, classed by the same rule. US adults: CDC NHANES August 2021–August 2023, adults aged 20 and over with a measured height and weight, pregnant women excluded, weighted with the exam weights; BMI, waist and height as CDC records them; 95% confidence intervals by Taylor linearization over the survey’s strata and primary sampling units, and the page states only differences whose interval excludes zero. The 2026 ADA thresholds are applied to the measured BMI, waist and height; adults identified in the survey as non-Hispanic Asian get the Asian thresholds; adults whose class depends on a waist that was not measured (54) are left out of that comparison, and the BMI-only share is computed on the same adults. The 600 dots are drawn by systematic sampling with probability proportional to the exam weight, from a shuffled list with a fixed seed.
