BMI Chart for Adults by Height and Weight

Written by Marko Visic, MPharm · Reviewed 27 September 2026 · Not medical advice

A BMI chart maps every height to the weight range that lands in each Body Mass Index category. Use this page as a quick reference: pick your height on the left, read across, and see whether your weight falls in the healthy band or another category. The chart works for adult men and women of any age.

A healthy BMI for adults is 18.5 to 24.9. At 5 ft 9 in that's 125–168 lb; 169–202 lb is overweight and 203 lb or more is obesity. The same cut-offs apply to adult men and women of all ages. Category cut-offs: CDC Adult BMI Categories.

How to read the chart

  1. Find your height on the left. Each row has a stable anchor (for example #h-5-9) so you can bookmark or share a link to your height.
  2. Read across to see the weight range for each BMI category at that height.
  3. Match your weight to the coloured band it falls into. Bands are inclusive of both endpoints.
  4. For the exact BMI number, use the BMI calculator.

What the chart can't tell you

BMI does not measure body fat directly. Very muscular people can read as overweight while carrying little fat, and body-fat distribution matters: fat stored around the abdomen is linked to higher cardiometabolic risk than fat on the hips. For context, see body fat vs BMI and BMI limitations.

  • Children and teens use age- and sex-specific percentiles, not the adult cut-offs. Use the pediatric BMI calculator.
  • Adults 65+: cut-offs are the same, but the observational risk pattern differs. See BMI by age.

Frequently asked questions

A healthy BMI is 18.5 to 24.9. The chart above shows the corresponding weight range at every height in one-inch and 5 cm steps. Pick your height row and read across to the healthy column. Category cut-offs are from the CDC. To see what US adults actually weigh at each height, see our average weight by height tables.

No. The CDC and WHO adult BMI cut-offs are the same for men and women. Women naturally carry a higher proportion of body fat than men at the same BMI, but the categories do not shift.

The CDC adult BMI cut-offs do not change with age. Children and teenagers are classified with age-specific percentiles instead of a fixed threshold. See the BMI by age guide for context on older adults.

Per the CDC, obesity begins at BMI 30. Class 1 obesity is 30 to less than 35, class 2 is 35 to less than 40, and class 3 (severe obesity) is 40 or greater. All three are shown in the chart above.

BMI equals weight in pounds times 703 divided by height in inches squared (or weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared). BMI is rounded to 0.1 half-up and then classified with the CDC ranges. Each weight band in the chart is the lowest to highest whole pound (or whole kilogram) that falls in that category at the given height.

Methodology and sources

  • Imperial formula: BMI = weight in pounds × 703 ÷ height in inches squared.
  • Metric formula: BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ height in metres squared.
  • Rounding rule: BMI to one decimal place, half-up; each weight takes the category of its rounded BMI.
  • Categories: CDC Adult BMI Categories.
