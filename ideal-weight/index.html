Key takeaways

Ideal body weight (IBW) is a weight worked out from height alone. The four formulas in use start at 5 feet and add a fixed amount per inch: for a 5′6″ woman they give 130 to 135 lb, inside the healthy BMI range of 115 to 154 lb at that height.

  • The formulas agree within 3 lb at 5′8″ for women and 5′6″ for men and split apart toward both ends: at 6′6″ the men’s run from 180 lb (Miller) to 214 lb (Hamwi).
  • At a medium frame every formula stays inside CDC’s healthy BMI range at the heights in the tables, at a BMI of 19.5 to 22.9 for women and 20.7 to 24.7 for men.
  • Hamwi (1964) works in pounds and adjusts for frame size: 10% less for a small frame, 10% more for a large one.
  • Pai and Paloucek concluded that “any one of these equations may be used to estimate IBW,” and Peterson and colleagues note that IBW equations “have largely been replaced by BMI ranges.”

Medical disclaimer: This calculator gives estimates for information only. BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis. Ask a healthcare professional for medical advice.

Hamwi’s rule, the oldest, comes from a 1964 chapter in an American Diabetes Association book: a desirable body weight in pounds, adjusted by 10% for frame size.

Devine’s equations (1974) come from an article on gentamicin dosing. Pai and Paloucek note that they “were not referenced to a particular data set” and trace them to a simple rule worked out from height–weight tables: 100 lb plus 5 lb an inch over 5 feet for women, 110 lb plus 5 lb for men. They don’t name its author.

Robinson’s team fitted its equations to the 1959 Metropolitan Life tables, Miller’s to the 1983 tables. Pai and Paloucek found them “strikingly similar” to Devine’s and concluded that “any one of these equations may be used to estimate IBW.”

Robinson’s abstract gives 51.65 kg + 1.85 kg (men) and 48.67 kg + 1.65 kg (women); the rounded values in Pai and Paloucek’s table are the ones in common use and in this calculator. All four start at 60 inches, so under 5 feet the calculator gives no formula weight.

Body frame size

Hamwi’s rule adds 10% for a large frame and takes off 10% for a small one: for a 5′6″ woman, 117 lb small, 130 lb medium, 143 lb large.

In the early height–weight tables, frame sizes “were not defined and were left to the subjective judgment of the examiner”; body frame was later defined in the 1959 Metropolitan table (Pai and Paloucek).

MedlinePlus reads frame size from the wrist:

MedlinePlus gives no cut-off for men 5′5″ and shorter. The cm values are our conversion.

A quicker test, from Davidson’s table: grasp the other wrist with the thumb and middle finger of your predominant hand. If they meet, the frame is medium; if they overlap appreciably, small; if they fail to meet, large.

In the calculator, frame size changes only Hamwi’s number.

Two worked examples

A 5′6″ woman, medium frame: 6 inches over 5 feet

Hamwi: 130 lb (59.0 kg)
100 + 5 × 6 = 130 lb
Devine: 131 lb (59.3 kg)
45.5 + 2.3 × 6 = 59.3 kg
Robinson: 131 lb (59.2 kg)
49 + 1.7 × 6 = 59.2 kg
Miller: 135 lb (61.3 kg)
53.1 + 1.36 × 6 = 61.26 kg

The four span 130 to 135 lb, a BMI of 21.0 to 21.8. The healthy range at 5′6″ is 115 to 154 lb (52 to 70 kg).

A 5′10″ man, medium and large frame: 10 inches over 5 feet

Hamwi: 166 lb (75.3 kg)
106 + 6 × 10 = 166 lb
Devine: 161 lb (73.0 kg)
50 + 2.3 × 10 = 73 kg
Robinson: 157 lb (71.0 kg)
52 + 1.9 × 10 = 71 kg
Miller: 155 lb (70.3 kg)
56.2 + 1.41 × 10 = 70.3 kg

The four span 155 to 166 lb, a BMI of 22.2 to 23.8, inside the healthy range of 129 to 173 lb (59 to 78 kg). A large frame changes only Hamwi’s: 166 × 1.1 = 182.6, so 183 lb (82.8 kg), a BMI of 26.2, above that range.

Ideal weight or a healthy weight range?

A formula gives one weight for a height. Peterson and colleagues note that “IBW equations predict a single target body weight, whereas most clinicians favor, and the empirical data support, a range of target body weights.” CDC’s adult BMI categories give that range: a healthy weight is a BMI of 18.5 to under 25, which at 5′6″ is 115 to 154 lb. The BMI chart lists it at every height, as do the charts for women and men.

Neither measure tells muscle from fat. CDC notes that “BMI does not distinguish between fat, muscle, and bone mass”; the lean body mass calculator and the body fat calculator estimate lean mass and body fat.

Clinicians still use IBW equations “to calculate drug dosing, to estimate overweight and underweight statuses, and to calculate nutrient intakes,” Peterson and colleagues write. This page doesn’t cover drug dosing.

Method. Every number in the tables, figure and examples is our calculation with the calculator’s arithmetic: the constants in the first table and 2.20462 lb per kg, from 60 inches up, with Hamwi’s weight × 0.9 for a small frame and × 1.1 for a large one. Weights print as whole pounds and kilograms to 0.1, halves up; the span runs from the lowest to the highest as printed. BMI is CDC’s formula at one decimal. Healthy ranges are the whole pounds and whole kilograms with a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9 at that height, each unit worked out on its own.
