Standard BMI Calculator

The standard Body Mass Index calculator for adults aged 20 and over. Input in metric or imperial; output is the number, the category, your healthy weight span at your height and where your BMI ranks among US adults.

Best for: Quick BMI screening for any adult

Example: A person who is 5'7" and weighs 160 lbs has a BMI of 25.1 (Overweight). Their healthy weight range at this height is 118–159 lbs.

BMI Calculator for Women

Ranks your BMI among US women and checks a waist measurement against the NIH, NICE and ADA thresholds.

Best for: Women who want context beyond the number: the US comparison and a waist check

Example: A woman who is 5'4" and weighs 140 lbs has a BMI of 24.0 (Healthy weight). She is within the healthy range (108–145 lbs for her height).

BMI Calculator by Age

Uses CDC’s adult categories (healthy 18.5–24.9) at every age from 20, ranks your BMI among US adults of your sex and age group, then adds a finding for adults 65 and over: compared with a BMI of 23 to 23.9, the risk of death was higher below 23 and began to rise at about 33 (Winter et al., Am J Clin Nutr, 2014).

Best for: Anyone who wants to see their BMI next to US averages for their age and sex

Example: A 65-year-old who is 5'6" and weighs 155 lbs has a standard BMI of 25.0. In a meta-analysis of adults 65 and over, the risk of death was higher below a BMI of 23 and began to rise at about 33 (Winter et al., Am J Clin Nutr, 2014); 25.0 lies between the two.

Pediatric BMI Calculator

Percentile-based, ages 2–19, sex-specific. Adult fixed cutoffs do not apply below 20. A child’s BMI is read against the CDC growth-chart distribution for their age and sex.

Best for: Parents of children ages 2–19

Example: A 10-year-old boy who is 4'6" and weighs 70 lbs has a BMI of 16.9, about the 54th percentile, a healthy weight for his age and sex.

New BMI Calculator (Trefethen)

Uses the 2013 Trefethen variant: 1.3 × weight (kg) ÷ height (m)^2.5. Applied at fixed weight, the two formulas cross near 1.69 m; short readers get a higher value under Trefethen, tall readers get a lower one.

Best for: Very short or very tall people who want a second look at their BMI

Example: A 6'2" person weighing 195 lbs has a traditional BMI of 25.0 but a New BMI of 23.7, 1.3 points lower. New BMI reads lower than traditional BMI above about 5'6.5" and higher below it.

Ideal Weight Calculator

Four formulas at once: Devine 1974, Robinson 1983, Miller 1983, Hamwi 1964. All agree closely around 5′6″–5′8″ and fan outward at extreme heights.

Best for: Anyone who wants the classic formula-based weight estimates side by side

Example: A 5'6" woman with a medium frame gets 130–135 lbs across the four clinical formulas (Devine 131 lbs, Robinson 131 lbs, Miller 135 lbs, Hamwi 130 lbs).

Lean Body Mass Calculator

Splits total body weight into lean mass and fat mass. Boer (1984) is the primary formula; James (1976) and Hume (1966) provide the comparison. For an average US woman (5′3.5″, 172 lb) the three agree within about 1.6 kg; for an average US man (5′9″, 199 lb) they differ by about 5.8 kg, mostly because the Hume equation gives lower values for men.

Best for: Anyone who wants lean mass estimated from height and weight with three published formulas

Example: A 5'8" male weighing 170 lbs has an estimated lean body mass of 128.5 lbs (58.3 kg, Boer formula), which leaves about 41 lbs (24.4%) as fat mass.
Try Calculator →

Body Fat Calculator

Estimates body fat percentage three ways: tape measure (Navy method), skinfold calipers (Jackson–Pollock) or height and weight (Deurenberg). It shows how far apart the methods land and ranks your result against CDC body scans of US adults.

Best for: Estimating body fat at home, or sanity-checking a smart-scale or caliper reading

Example: A 35-year-old man, 5'10", with a 15.5-inch neck and a 35-inch waist comes out at 18.5% by the tape method; 94% of US men aged 30–39 have more body fat on CDC scans. See every age group on the body fat percentage chart.

How to Choose the Right Calculator

Not sure which tool to use? This decision guide will help you pick the most relevant calculator for your situation. You can always use more than one for a fuller picture.

For the most complete health picture, consider using 2–3 calculators together. For example, pair the Standard BMI with the Lean Body Mass calculator and your Ideal Weight range, or estimate your body fat with the Body Fat Calculator.

Understanding BMI Categories

The World Health Organization defines the following BMI categories for adults. These ranges are the same for women and men, though health risks at each level may differ by sex, age, and ethnicity. For a detailed breakdown, see our BMI calculator guide.

CategoryBMI Range (kg/m²)
Severe Thinness< 16.0
Moderate Thinness16.0 – 16.9
Mild Thinness17.0 – 18.4
Normal Weight18.5 – 24.9
Overweight25.0 – 29.9
Obese Class I30.0 – 34.9
Obese Class II35.0 – 39.9
Obese Class III≥ 40.0
Thinness grades: World Health Organization. Physical status: the use and interpretation of anthropometry. Technical Report Series 854. Geneva: WHO; 1995.

Source: World Health Organization, Obesity and Overweight Fact Sheet. For Asian populations, a WHO expert consultation identified BMI 23 and 27.5 as public-health action points.

Frequently Asked Questions

For a quick check, start with the Standard BMI Calculator, which also ranks your BMI among US adults. The Women and Men versions rank it among US women or men, and the women’s version checks a waist measurement too. The BMI by Age calculator compares you with US adults of your sex and age group. For children and teens aged 2 to 19, use the Pediatric BMI Calculator: it reads BMI as a percentile on CDC’s growth charts instead of the adult cut-offs. The New BMI Calculator shows Trefethen’s proposed formula next to the standard one; he writes that it may not be an improvement (Trefethen). For body composition, the Body Fat and Lean Body Mass calculators estimate fat and lean mass.

Each calculator uses a published, named formula, and the categories follow CDC and WHO; the arithmetic is exact. The limits are BMI’s own: CDC calls it a screening measure that “does not distinguish between fat, muscle, and bone mass” and says that for an individual it should be considered with other factors, such as blood pressure and cholesterol levels (CDC). At a given BMI, body fat may vary by sex, age, and race and Hispanic origin, NCHS notes (Health E-Stat 111).

The formula and the adult categories are the same: CDC applies its adult categories regardless of age, sex, or race (CDC). Body composition differs: on CDC’s body scans of adults aged 20 to 59 with a healthy BMI, women had a median of 33.4% body fat and men 22.1% (our calculation; see the body fat percentage chart). The NIH waist cut-offs differ too: more than 35 inches (88 cm) for women and 40 inches (102 cm) for men (NHLBI). The women’s and men’s calculators add that context.

The Standard, Women, Men, BMI by Age and New BMI calculators are for adults aged 20 and over, the ages CDC’s adult categories cover; the BMI by Age calculator also covers the research on adults 65 and over. The Pediatric BMI Calculator is for children and teens aged 2 through 19, the ages of CDC’s BMI-for-age charts. The Ideal Weight and Lean Body Mass calculators use formulas for adults.

At the NFL Scouting Combine, 53.4% of prospects had a BMI of 30 or more, but only 8.9% had obesity by measured body fat (Provencher et al., 2018). A body fat estimate from the Body Fat Calculator or a waist measurement tells you more; BMI for athletes covers the research.
