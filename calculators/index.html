Standard BMI Calculator

The classic Body Mass Index calculator for adults 18+. Input in metric or imperial; output is the number, the WHO category, and your healthy weight span at your height.

Best for: Quick BMI screening for any adult

Example: A person who is 5'7" and weighs 160 lbs has a BMI of 25.1 — Overweight. Their healthy weight range at this height is 118–159 lbs.

Best for: Men, especially those with higher muscle mass

BMI Calculator by Age

Uses the WHO adult 18.5–24.9 range for all adults, then adds the Winter 2014 observational finding: the BMI band with the lowest observed mortality in adults 65+ is 23–33.

Best for: Adults over 45 and seniors seeking age-specific guidance

Example: A 65-year-old who is 5'6" and weighs 155 lbs has a standard BMI of 25.0. With age adjustment, this falls within the observed lower-mortality band for adults 65+ (23–33 in Winter 2014), per the Winter 2014 meta-analysis.

Pediatric BMI Calculator

Percentile-based, ages 2–19, sex-specific. Adult fixed cutoffs do not apply below 20 — a child’s BMI is read against the CDC growth-chart distribution for their age and sex.

Best for: Parents of children ages 2–19

Example: A 10-year-old boy who is 4'6" and weighs 70 lbs has a BMI of 16.8, placing him at approximately the 50th percentile — Healthy Weight for his age and sex.
Try Calculator →

New BMI Calculator (Trefethen)

Uses the 2013 Trefethen variant: 1.3 × weight (kg) ÷ height (m)^2.5. Applied at fixed weight, the two formulas cross near 1.69 m; short readers get a higher value under Trefethen, tall readers get a lower one.

Best for: Very short or tall individuals curious about height bias correction

Example: A 6'2" person weighing 195 lbs has a traditional BMI of 25.0 but a New BMI of 24.3 — the traditional formula slightly overestimates BMI for taller individuals. The difference narrows for average-height people.
Try Calculator → Read the Guide

Ideal Weight Calculator

Four formulas at once: Devine 1974, Robinson 1983, Miller 1983, Hamwi 1964. All agree closely around 5′6″–5′8″ and fan outward at extreme heights.

Best for: Anyone seeking a clinically-backed target weight range

Example: A 5'6" woman with a medium frame has an ideal weight range of approximately 120–144 lbs across the four clinical formulas (Devine: 130 lbs, Robinson: 136 lbs, Miller: 126 lbs, Hamwi: 130 lbs).
Try Calculator →

Lean Body Mass Calculator

Splits total body weight into lean mass and fat mass. Boer (1984) is the primary formula; James (1976) and Hume (1966) provide the comparison — typical spread is under 2 kg for average bodies, wider for heavier individuals.

Best for: Athletes, fitness enthusiasts, and anyone tracking body composition

Example: A 5'8" male weighing 170 lbs has an estimated lean body mass of approximately 141 lbs (Boer formula), meaning about 29 lbs (~17%) of his weight is fat — within the healthy range for men.

How to Choose the Right Calculator

Not sure which tool to use? This decision guide will help you pick the most relevant calculator for your situation. You can always use more than one for a fuller picture.

If you want to... Use this calculator Why
Get a quick BMI check Standard BMI The fastest way to calculate your BMI using the WHO formula
Get women-specific guidance BMI for Women Accounts for higher natural body fat and provides life-stage guidance
Get men-specific guidance BMI for Men Addresses muscle mass, abdominal fat patterns, and male health thresholds
Factor in your age (especially 45+) BMI by Age Provides age-adjusted healthy ranges — a slightly higher BMI may be protective for seniors
Calculate BMI for a child (ages 2–19) Pediatric BMI Uses CDC growth chart percentiles instead of fixed adult cutoffs
Correct for height bias (very short/tall) New BMI (Trefethen) Uses height^2.5 instead of height^2 to produce fairer results across different heights
Find a target weight goal Ideal Weight Compares four clinical formulas (Devine, Robinson, Miller, Hamwi) for a realistic range
Analyze body composition (muscle vs. fat) Lean Body Mass Estimates lean mass and body fat percentage — ideal for athletes and fitness tracking

For the most complete health picture, consider using 2–3 calculators together. For example, pair the Standard BMI with the Lean Body Mass calculator and your Ideal Weight range.

Understanding BMI Categories

The World Health Organization defines the following BMI categories for adults. These ranges are the same for women and men, though health risks at each level may differ by sex, age, and ethnicity. For a detailed breakdown, see our BMI calculator guide.

CategoryBMI Range (kg/m²)
Severe Thinness< 16.0
Moderate Thinness16.0 – 16.9
Mild Thinness17.0 – 18.4
Normal Weight18.5 – 24.9
Overweight25.0 – 29.9
Obese Class I30.0 – 34.9
Obese Class II35.0 – 39.9
Obese Class III≥ 40.0

Source: World Health Organization — Obesity and Overweight Fact Sheet. For Asian populations, overweight begins at BMI 23 and obesity at 27.5.

Frequently Asked Questions

For a quick health screening, start with the Standard BMI Calculator. If you want gender-specific interpretation, use the Women or Men version. Adults over 45 should try the BMI by Age calculator for age-adjusted healthy ranges, as research suggests a slightly higher BMI may be protective for older adults. Parents should use the Pediatric BMI Calculator for children ages 2–19 — it uses CDC growth chart percentiles rather than fixed adult cutoffs. If you are very short or very tall, the New BMI Calculator corrects the traditional formula's height bias. For body composition analysis, combine the Ideal Weight and Lean Body Mass calculators.

Our calculators use the same WHO-endorsed formulas that healthcare providers use worldwide. The mathematical calculations are precise. However, BMI itself has well-known limitations as a health metric: it cannot distinguish between muscle and fat, does not account for fat distribution, and may be less accurate for certain ethnic groups. BMI is most useful as a population-level screening tool rather than an individual diagnostic. For the most complete assessment, we recommend combining your BMI with other measurements such as waist circumference, body fat percentage (see our Lean Body Mass Calculator), and standard blood work from your healthcare provider.

The BMI formula itself is identical for men and women — BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)² regardless of sex. The WHO classification ranges (18.5–24.9 = Normal, etc.) are also the same. However, the health implications differ because men and women have fundamentally different body compositions. Women naturally carry 6–11% more body fat than men at the same BMI. Men tend to store fat abdominally, which is associated with higher cardiovascular risk. This is why our Women's BMI Calculator and Men's BMI Calculator provide tailored interpretation and guidance, even though the underlying BMI number is calculated the same way.

The Standard, Women, Men, and New BMI calculators are designed for adults aged 20 and older. The BMI by Age Calculator covers all adults but is especially valuable for those over 45, where age-adjusted ranges may apply. The Pediatric BMI Calculator is specifically for children and teens ages 2–19 and uses CDC growth chart percentiles. For infants under 2, BMI is not recommended — weight-for-length charts should be used instead under pediatrician guidance. The Ideal Weight and Lean Body Mass calculators are intended for adults of all ages.

You can, but be aware that BMI frequently misclassifies athletes and muscular individuals. Because BMI cannot distinguish between muscle and fat, a football player with a BMI of 30 and 12% body fat is categorized the same as a sedentary person with 35% body fat. For athletes, the Lean Body Mass Calculator is the most appropriate tool, as it estimates your fat-free mass and body fat percentage. You can also use the New BMI Calculator, which may produce slightly different (and sometimes more accurate) results for taller athletes. For a target weight, the Ideal Weight Calculator offers a useful reference, though athletes in strength sports will typically exceed these ranges due to muscle mass.

For adults with stable weight, checking your BMI a few times per year is sufficient. If you are actively trying to lose or gain weight, monthly checks can help track progress without causing unnecessary stress over day-to-day fluctuations. Daily weight can vary by 2–4 lbs due to water retention and food intake, so always weigh yourself at the same time of day (ideally morning) for consistency. For children, BMI should be tracked at regular pediatric checkups, typically annually. Focus on long-term trends rather than individual readings — a gradual change over months is more meaningful than a single measurement. If you notice a consistent upward or downward trend, consult your healthcare provider.
